How it was built

From messy inputs to defensible outputs.

The project is intentionally documented as a sequence of engineering decisions, experiments, and failure checks rather than a single headline score.

01 · The problem

A used-car decision is two different questions.

Listings can estimate asking price, but they cannot explain ownership experience. I kept numerical market context separate from subjective review evidence.

02 · Price intelligence

Train on earlier listings. Test on later ones.

CatBoost trained on 238,959 top-10-make listings using a chronological 70/15/15 split. Feature engineering reduced final test MAE to $2,866 with R² = 0.883.

03 · Owner intelligence

Search first. Generate second.

137,660 raw reviews became 102,546 cleaned reviews; 70,665 matched the price-model scope. MiniLM + FAISS retrieves vehicle-specific evidence before Qwen sees the question.

04 · Grounding

A fluent answer is not automatically trustworthy.

Generated citations are checked against retrieved review IDs. If citation grounding fails, the response falls back to source excerpts. The final demo exercised that exact path.

Experiment history

The final system came from what failed.

R0MiniLM + FAISS

Fast dense-retrieval baseline over the matched owner-review corpus.

R1Metadata filtering

Vehicle make/model-family/year constraints reduced “right topic, wrong car” retrieval.

R2IR evaluation

Hit@1/3/5, MRR@10 and nDCG@10 replaced a single hit-rate with a ranking-quality panel.

G1Grounding fallback

When Qwen failed the citation check, the interface returned retrieved source excerpts instead.

Evaluation note: Hit@5, MRR and nDCG here are identity-retrieval sanity metrics from 60 sampled queries. They are useful for this corpus, but they are not directly comparable to BEIR/MS MARCO leaderboards. Price context is based on 2021 Craigslist asking prices, not completed sale prices.

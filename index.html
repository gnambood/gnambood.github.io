STORY 01 / GRADUATE RESEARCH

Can a few seconds of video reveal subtle signs of driver impairment?

My graduate research at Western University's AiX Lab explored whether synchronized RGB and infrared facial video could distinguish sober, low-impairment and severe-impairment sessions.

Python / PyTorch / Computer vision
TimeSformer / VideoMAE
Participant-separated evaluation
01 · The challenge

Real video isn't a clean dataset

Facial movement and appearance vary with lighting, camera angle and recording quality. Before modelling, the source sessions had to be checked for the face visibility and camera consistency the experiment needed. From the original 60 participants, the final publication cohort retained 57 after source-quality exclusions.

02 · The exploration

Try different ways to understand time

TimeSformer, Video Swin and ViViT were part of the architecture exploration. The retained publication work then focused on a reference-free TimeSformer system and a separate personalized VideoMAE approach. Not every early exploratory run has preserved, directly comparable results, so I don't present a made-up leaderboard.

03 · The turning point

How I cropped the face mattered

A controlled TimeSformer crop comparison was a reminder that representation choices can outweigh architectural novelty.

44.44%Crop A accuracy in matched replay
76.02%Crop B accuracy in matched replay

The final reference-free pipeline used full-face/head-motion Crop B and synchronized RGB/IR inputs, sampling 16 frames from each preserved 125-frame clip.

04 · The evaluation

Keep people separate across folds

The reported TimeSformer result covers 57 participants and 171 sessions across five participant-disjoint outer folds and three training seeds. The preserved evaluation reports 76.02% accuracy and 76.05% macro-F1. It is a frozen downstream replay on the accepted feature lineage, not a claim of byte-identical end-to-end regeneration.

05 · The parallel experiment

What if you know someone's sober baseline?

VideoMAE investigated a different condition, using same-participant sober calibration. Its frozen post-hoc result is 66.08% accuracy. Because the inference conditions and evaluation details differ, I do not rank it directly against the reference-free TimeSformer system.

76.05%TimeSformer macro-F1

Manuscript in preparation. Research code and participant data are private. The website reports the accepted evaluation, including its reproducibility boundaries.

What this project taught me

It reinforced that experimental design begins before training: with what is visible in the data, how it is cropped, who is allowed into a held-out fold and what a result actually supports. A more complex model isn't automatically a more defensible result.

STORY 02 / END-TO-END ML APPLICATION

Buying a used car raises two very different questions.

How much is it asking for in the historical listing market? And what do owners say it is like to live with? Auto Market Assistant keeps these questions separate instead of forcing one model to answer both.

CatBoost / scikit-learn
MiniLM / FAISS / FastAPI
Docker / AWS / Parquet
01 · The pricing problem

Estimate first, explain the context

I trained CatBoost on 238,959 historical Craigslist listing records and used a chronological holdout instead of treating later listings as interchangeable with earlier ones. The result was a test MAE of $2,866 and R² of 0.883. These are asking prices from 2021, not today's market values or completed sales.

02 · The owner experience

Retrieve evidence rather than invent certainty

A second system embeds matched Edmunds owner reviews using MiniLM, then searches them with FAISS. It indexes 70,665 matched reviews. The recorded vehicle-identity retrieval benchmark reports Hit@5 of 81.67%; evidence gates and citation validation prevent weak support from being presented as a confident answer.

03 · From notebook to application

Two models, one usable workflow

I refactored the data cleaning into validated Parquet pipelines, built a FastAPI service and packaged the application with Docker. Qwen 2.5 generation was tested separately, while the deployed AWS service was configured to return deterministic retrieval-backed evidence.

2021 listings→CatBoost→PriceOwner reviews→MiniLM / FAISS→Cited evidence
04 · A decision not to overpromise

Not enough history to forecast responsibly

The inspected source dates span roughly one month in 2021. That is not a sound basis for claiming reliable multi-month price forecasts. Instead, I built descriptive price and listing-volume trends by comparable make, model family and vehicle year, with sample-size gates and lineage checks.

05 · The way you can try it

Show genuine saved results without a cloud bill

The portfolio's interactive case study runs entirely in the browser, replaying a verified saved CatBoost result and cited review IDs. It is not live inference. The actual AWS ECS service has been deployed, but compute is paused to limit cost. The canonical GitHub repository retains the technical implementation.

What this project taught me

A useful system needs more than a model: it needs a reproducible data definition, a trustworthy explanation of what its metrics measure and an interface that doesn't claim more than the evidence supports.

STORY 03 / EXPLORATORY SECURITY ML

What if a risk signal is scattered across an ordinary working day?

Insider Threat Detection began as a question about patterns in employee activity logs: whether information spread across many event types could help distinguish suspicious behaviour from routine activity.

Python / Feature engineering
Random Forest / SVM
Experimental evaluation
01 · The challenge

Events don't arrive as one neat story

Simulated enterprise activity includes logs and device, file and communication records. The first challenge is turning scattered events into features a supervised classifier can interpret without forgetting the original activity context.

02 · The experiment

Compare simple and nonlinear models

The notebook compares Logistic Regression, an RBF SVM and Random Forest. Its saved Random Forest output records 99.04% accuracy and approximately 0.97 macro-F1. These are historical notebook results, not independently reproduced production performance.

03 · The lesson

A high score is a question, not the end of the story

Before treating these metrics as a deployment claim, the experimental unit, class balance, splitting policy and leakage controls still need a dedicated audit. I keep that limitation visible rather than dressing an exploratory notebook up as a security product.

STORY 01 / GRADUATE RESEARCH

Can a few seconds of video reveal subtle signs of driver impairment?

My graduate research at Western University's AiX Lab explored whether synchronized RGB and infrared facial video could distinguish sober, low-impairment and severe-impairment sessions.

Python / PyTorch / Computer vision
TimeSformer / VideoMAE
Participant-separated evaluation
01 · The challenge

Real video isn't a clean dataset

Facial movement and appearance vary with lighting, camera angle and recording quality. Before modelling, the source sessions had to be checked for the face visibility and camera consistency the experiment needed. From the original 60 participants, the final publication cohort retained 57 after source-quality exclusions.

02 · The exploration

Try different ways to understand time

TimeSformer, Video Swin and ViViT were part of the architecture exploration. The retained publication work then focused on a reference-free TimeSformer system and a separate personalized VideoMAE approach. Not every early exploratory run has preserved, directly comparable results, so I don't present a made-up leaderboard.

03 · The turning point

How I cropped the face mattered

A controlled TimeSformer crop comparison was a reminder that representation choices can outweigh architectural novelty.

44.44%Crop A accuracy in matched replay
76.02%Crop B accuracy in matched replay

The final reference-free pipeline used full-face/head-motion Crop B and synchronized RGB/IR inputs, sampling 16 frames from each preserved 125-frame clip.

04 · The evaluation

Keep people separate across folds

The reported TimeSformer result covers 57 participants and 171 sessions across five participant-disjoint outer folds and three training seeds. The preserved evaluation reports 76.02% accuracy and 76.05% macro-F1. It is a frozen downstream replay on the accepted feature lineage, not a claim of byte-identical end-to-end regeneration.

05 · The parallel experiment

What if you know someone's sober baseline?

VideoMAE investigated a different condition, using same-participant sober calibration. Its frozen post-hoc result is 66.08% accuracy. Because the inference conditions and evaluation details differ, I do not rank it directly against the reference-free TimeSformer system.

Manuscript in preparation. Research code and participant data are private. The website reports the accepted evaluation, including its reproducibility boundaries.

What this project taught me

It reinforced that experimental design begins before training: with what is visible in the data, how it is cropped, who is allowed into a held-out fold and what a result actually supports. A more complex model isn't automatically a more defensible result.
